02

How a line gets its price.

The same four steps on every line, whether it’s a $12 gasket or a $12,000 order of supplies.

  1. 1

    Read the whole requisition

    Every attachment, every page — spec sheets, sample photos, the bid form. A picture on page three can change which item is right, so nothing gets skipped.

  2. 2

    Find the exact item

    A part number or UPC has to match. Pack sizes and quantities are checked against what you actually asked for, so twelve means twelve, not twelve boxes.

  3. 3

    Check the real cart

    We put the item in the supplier’s cart and go through checkout to the last screen before payment, to read the true shipping and tax for your facility.

  4. 4

    Quote it delivered

    One price per unit with shipping inside it, entered on BidBuy with the signed bid form. When a purchase order comes, we order and track it to your dock.

03

Five rules we don’t bend.

  1. i.

    A part number means that part.

    If the line names a part, we quote that part — or tell you we couldn’t find it. We don’t slip in a look-alike.

  2. ii.

    “Or equal” only when you wrote it.

    An equivalent is offered only when the requisition allows one, and it has to match the named item’s form — same pack, same size, same spec.

  3. iii.

    Shipping is in the price.

    The number you see is the number you pay. Heavy or bulky freight is estimated honestly and flagged, never left out.

  4. iv.

    Nothing is priced on a guess.

    A price comes from a real cart or a real quote, and the evidence for every line is kept.

  5. v.

    If we can’t do it right, we No Bid.

    Services, food and dealer-only equipment aren’t ours to quote. A clean No Bid beats a quote we can’t stand behind.
